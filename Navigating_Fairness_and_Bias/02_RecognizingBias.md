Representation bias limits who the model can see accurately, while measurement bias distorts how the model interprets what it sees. Together, they reveal that AI systems are not neutral, rather, they mirror the values, assumptions, and blind spots of the societies that create them. 

Representation bias occurs when the data used to train a model fails to reflect the diversity of the real world. When certain groups are underrepresented, or entirely absent, the model learns patterns that work well only for the groups that are included. The Gender Shade Project illustrates this idea clearly. Joy Buolamwini and Timnit Gebru found that commercial facial analysis systems performed best on lighter-skinned men and worst on darker-skinned women. This wasn't because the algorithms inherently "preferred" one group over another; rather, the training datasets contained far more images of lighter-skinned individuals. As a result, the systems learned to recognize those faces more accurately. The documentary Coded Bias expands on this idea, showing how underrepresentation in datasets can lead to real-world misidentification, discrimination, and unequal access to services. When the data doesn't represent everyone, the technology doesn't serve everyone.

Measurement bias arises when the features or labels used to train a model are themselves flawed, inconsistent, or based on subjective judgements. Even if a dataset is diverse, the way information is measured or categorized can introduce bias. In Stop Assuming Data, Algorithms, and AI are objective, Haggis-Burridge emphasizes that data collection is a human process shaped by assumptions, simplifications, and cultural norms. For example, using binary gender labels in facial analysis systems imposes a narrow definition of gender that does not reflect human complexity. This is a form of measurement bias, as the categories themselves distort reality. Similarly, Coded Bias highlights how algorithmic systems often rely on proxies, such as ZIP codes for creditworthiness, that embed structural inequalities into the model's predictions.
